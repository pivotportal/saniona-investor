About Saniona

Saniona is a drug-development company with a research platform focused on ion channels. The platform is used to identify and develop drug candidates and has led to collaborations with external pharmaceutical companies.

Saniona currently has six partner collaborations, including Jazz Pharmaceuticals and Acadia Pharmaceuticals, plus the spin-out Initiator Pharma. The collaborations include funding of parts of the research and development work. The combined value of the agreements amounts to over USD 1.8 billion, provided that all agreed are met. The company reported a positive result in both 2024 and 2025.

Saniona also develops its own drug candidates. Around the turn of the year 2026/2027, the first human clinical studies are planned for two of the company’s candidates. SAN2668 is being developed to treat developmental and epileptic encephalopathies () in children. SAN2465 is being developed to treat major depressive disorder (), with as the planned focus of Phase 2. The company’s financing is expected to last into 2029, covering the planned studies without an immediate need for new capital.

Board of directors

The board has four members. Since 2025 the chairman is John Haurum, who brings broad operational and commercial biotech experience (former CEO of F-star and co-founder of Symphogen). Founder Jørgen Drejer serves as deputy chairman. The other members are Anna Ljung (CEO of Moberg Pharma) and Carl Johan Sundberg (professor at Karolinska Institutet).

Vision

“To improve the lives of epilepsy patients around the world through scientific innovation.” Its mission is to leverage the company’s ion-channel expertise to discover, develop and deliver innovative treatments.

Research platform

All of Saniona’s work builds on its own ion-channel platform. At its core is the IONBASE database, with structure-activity data on more than 130,000 compounds, over 25,000 of them the company’s own. The platform has produced five clinical candidates in five years and underpins the deals with Jazz, Acadia, Boehringer Ingelheim and AstronauTx, among others.

Internal wholly-owned programs

Full upside, but Saniona carries the cost and risk

Q1 2027first-in-human dosing

The lead candidate and one of the two programs closest to the clinic. Potentially first in its class for children with hard-to-treat, often drug-resistant epilepsy. The company sees potential to develop SAN2668 broadly across several syndromes rather than a single indication. According to the Q2 report, the rare-disease focus may allow Saniona to keep the program through late-stage development and commercialise it on its own.

SAN2668 is, as a starting point, “not for sale”, as the company sees an opportunity to take the program all the way to a registration package itself.

α2/α3-selective (PAM) with balanced α1. The idea is to keep benzodiazepine-class seizure control with less sedation, cognitive impact and dependence

Compound patent into the 2040s
data (Saniona presentation)

Better seizure control than clobazam at clinically comparable exposure, with no motor impairment and no sedation. Reduces spike-wave discharges (SWD) in three genetic DEE models: fully in SYNGAP1 and STXBP1, roughly by half in CHD2. A strong reduction is also seen in the GAERS rat model. It also improves symptoms beyond seizures, such as cognition, aggression and social interaction. Adds to the effect of lamotrigine, levetiracetam and valproate.

Mechanism
(NAM) of GABAA α5. A new mechanism, different from standard antidepressants, NMDA antagonists and psychedelics
Patent and deal
Compound patent into the 2040s
Preclinical data (Saniona presentation)

Anhedonia normalised within 24 hours and sustained for at least 48 hours after a single oral dose; anxiety-like behaviour fully reversed. Effect size comparable to ketamine, where conventional antidepressants need 3–5 weeks of daily dosing. Restores cognition across two domains and two independent models (comparable to ketamine/donepezil) with no effect on locomotor activity. Anatomically restricted target engagement (hippocampus, ventral striatum and amygdala) confirmed by PET in non-human primates.

Risks

Risks to weigh

A balanced view means seeing the risks, not only the opportunities.

Clinical risk

The lead programs are still preclinical. The step into Phase 1 and beyond is big, and most drug candidates never reach the market.

Dependence on partners

A large share of value sits in partner-driven programs (Jazz, Acadia and others). Saniona does not control their timelines or decisions, and milestones may slip or fail to materialise.

Need for future deals

SAN2465 (depression) is likely too large for Saniona to take through late-stage trials alone. Its value probably depends on an out-licensing deal that does not yet exist.

Financing & dilution

The cash lasts into 2029 under the current plan. Delays or bigger ambitions could still require new capital, which could dilute shareholders.

Regulatory uncertainty

Approvals (e.g. tesofensine in Mexico) are set by authorities with no fixed timelines. Both outcome and timing are uncertain.

Market & liquidity risk

This is a small-cap stock and can be volatile. The share price is affected by news flow, sentiment and limited trading liquidity.
